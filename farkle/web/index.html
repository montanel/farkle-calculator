About the numbers

Turns to win How many turns, on average, the set needs to reach the wager you pick above the table, playing each turn optimally for that wager — banking the instant a pass would cross the line rather than chasing points. This is the number sets are ranked by, lowest first, and it already prices in busting: a Farkle banks nothing yet still costs a turn, so a reckless set climbs here even when its points look good.
Points / turn The mean banked in one turn under unlimited points-max play, computed exactly — no simulation. A rare tail of hot-dice runs drags it well above a typical turn, so read it as a diagnostic, not a score to expect.
Turn spread Open a set to see how that mean breaks down: the median turn, the middle-half range, how often a turn busts (banks nothing), and what it averages when it banks. For strong, aggressive sets the median sits far below the mean — that gap is exactly why the mean alone misleads.
Optimal strategy Click any set to see when to throw and when to pass, or use Play along to enter each throw as you make it and be told exactly which dice to set aside. Play along is need-aware: tell it the wager and your score so far and it plays for the win — banking the instant you cover the remaining need, not chasing points past the line.
Ordinary dice The game fills any slot you do not fill yourself, so sets using fewer than six special dice are considered automatically.
Caveat Turns to win assumes a solo race to a fixed wager — it plays for the target, not against an opponent, so it does not yet account for a rival who might reach the line first.
